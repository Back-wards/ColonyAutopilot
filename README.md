# Colony Autopilot

**Place a Town Hall. The colony runs the rest.**

Colony Autopilot is an addon for [MineColonies](https://www.curseforge.com/minecraft/mc-mods/minecolonies) on Minecraft 1.21.1 (NeoForge). It plays the town planner for you: it picks where every building goes, shapes the land, lays lit roads, orders builds and upgrades, hires and equips the citizens, runs the university, repairs raid damage and mans the defences. Your citizens still do the work, by MineColonies' own rules. You keep the parts of MineColonies that are fun: the quests, the raids, the treasury, and watching a village grow out of nothing.

Made by **Backwards** ([CurseForge](https://www.curseforge.com/members/backwards/projects)). Licensed under GPL-3.0.

- **Download the mod:** [Colony Autopilot on CurseForge](https://www.curseforge.com/minecraft/mc-mods/colonyautopilot)
- **Want the full apocalypse?** The [AutoColony Apocalypse](https://www.curseforge.com/minecraft/modpacks/autocolony-apocalypse) modpack bundles this mod with Fungal Infection: Spore, guns, turrets and a 50-day countdown to the end of the world.
- **Bugs and questions:** [open an issue here](https://github.com/Back-wards/ColonyAutopilot/issues). Please do not report Colony Autopilot problems to the MineColonies team.

## What you need

- Minecraft 1.21.1 with NeoForge 21.1 (any 21.1.x build; the mod is built against 21.1.235 and tested on 21.1.251 and 21.1.267).
- MineColonies 1.1.1403 or newer, with the libraries it already asks for (Structurize, BlockUI, Domum Ornamentum). Tested with 1.1.1403 and 1.1.1420; the table at the end of this page says what "tested" means.
- Put `colonyautopilot-<version>.jar` in the `mods` folder. On a server it goes on the server **and** on every player's client, the same version everywhere.

It works on a colony you already have. If you ever want to remove the mod, run `/colonyautopilot off` first so the colony hands its buildings back to plain MineColonies.

### Coming from an older version

- **Start a new world.** Each version is made for a new world; nothing converts a world played on an older one.
- **Delete the old growth plan file.** `config/colonyautopilot-growthplan.json` is written only when it is missing, and it belongs to the install, not to the world: an install that ran an older version keeps its old plan in every world, new ones included. Delete the file (or use a fresh install) and the current plan is written in its place. Example: a plan file written by 1.3.4 has no Stable step, so no Stable is built and the village never gets cavalry until the file is gone.
- **A Town Hall that 1.3.4 built a second hall into** (an abandoned colony's hall you took over, or a hall of another style than the one you picked) stays that way in the old world, and the repair keeps putting the wrong blocks back. The way out: switch **Auto repair damage** off for that colony (the Repair section of its settings page, or `/colonyautopilot colony <id> set repair.autoRepairDamage false`), remove the blocks that were pasted in, all of them, then switch it on again. The hall is then left as it stands. While the switch is off no other building of that colony is repaired either. (Run on the test server with a generated Caledonia hall; version 1.4 no longer pastes into such a hall in the first place.)

## Your first hour

1. **Join the world.** On your first join you get a starter kit: MineColonies' build tool, a **Zone Marker** (while the server has protected zones on), and, with GuideME installed, the **Field Guide**, the in-game book that explains everything on this page in detail. The first player to join a world that has no colony yet also gets a **Town Hall** block, once per world.
2. **Place the Town Hall and confirm the founding** in the window that opens. From this moment the colony is on autopilot.
3. **Watch the village start.** Within a game-minute the first Builder's Hut is placed on flat ground near the hall, its ground levelled and a lamp-lit road laid to its door. The builder gets the materials delivered and starts (in Progression Mode, once the treasury can pay for them: step 5). The next building follows ten game-minutes later at the earliest, and in Progression Mode it also waits for a free builder: two more Builder's Huts, so three crews work from the start, then the colony's first guard post, the Barracks. After that the plan waits until the Town Hall itself is built. Then come a Tavern for recruits, Residences, a Guard Tower, a Farm, a Warehouse, a Blacksmith's Hut, a Courier's Hut, and on through a 70-step plan that ends in a fully grown town. (From the Tavern on, that is the order of a new growth-plan file. A file left by an older version keeps the order it was written with: see *Coming from an older version* above.)
4. **Read the chat.** The colony tells you when a building finishes, when the town grows, when the Town Hall goes up a level, and once a day it lists anything that needs a human. `/colonyautopilot status` shows what each colony is doing right now.
5. **Keep the treasury full.** In Progression Mode (on by default) the colony pays for its supplies in **ColonyBucks**, a coin you earn by playing. More on that below. If you would rather have the colony supplied for free, switch Progression Mode off on the town hall's Autopilot settings page.

## What the colony does for you

- **Finds or makes the land.** Flat, dry plots first. When none fit, it levels the ground or carves the footprint, so no building is skipped. A levelled plot is cleared of whatever stands on it, your own blocks included (only a column that holds a chest, a furnace, a sign or another such block, or a block nothing can break such as bedrock, is spared): mark a zone over a wall or a garden you want kept. A ravine between buildings gets a causeway.
- **Builds around what the world generated.** A village, a ruin or a tower standing at the surface near the colony is left as it is: no building, levelling, road, field, pond or lamp lands on it. Example: found a colony beside a desert pyramid and the roads go round it. A structure stops being left alone once any building of the colony stands on its ground, the open ground between its houses included: found your Town Hall inside a village, or place a hut there yourself, and that village is the colony's to build in. Only a building that finds no other ground at all may take a structure's ground. Buried structures and ones floating in the sky are not counted, and one made with `/place` is not seen. The switch is **Avoid structures**, per colony (`growth.avoidStructures`). A server owner can name structures the colony should treat as ordinary ground in `growth.structuresIgnored`: add `minecraft:ruined_portal` there and roads may run over a ruined portal.
- **Builds along a plan and upgrades in order.** The Town Hall goes up as soon as the population allows it, nothing climbs more than one level above the hall, and upgrades of standing buildings come before new ones. Any of the colony's Builder's Huts may take any of its building jobs its level allows, however far from the hut (MineColonies alone allows only 100 blocks), and the nearest free builder gets it; the colony still places its other new buildings within 100 blocks of a staffed Builder's Hut. Eight buildings are never given up on: the Tavern, the Warehouse, the Blacksmith's Hut, the Dining Hall, the University, the Stable (where the colony's style pack ships one), the Library and the Hospital, each once its research is done. When one is the plan's next building, new guard towers (never the village's first) and extra Builder's Huts wait for it, and it is placed even while everyone has a job. Where no plot fits it is searched farther out, then at the claim's edge, then near good land out of your builders' reach, for which a new Builder's Hut is raised when a citizen is free to work there (that land is kept off other buildings until the building is placed), then on levelled or carved ground (with terraforming on). While no ground can be found at all it is tried again every 40 game-minutes and later steps go on.
- **Roads and light.** A lamp-lit road from every door into the village, laid again when it breaks, with lamp posts spaced so they light the road without crowding it. Where a road steps diagonally, the corner block between the two steps is laid as road too where nothing stands in the way: it used to be left as it stood, and over a crack in the ground that left an open hole in the road.
- **Hires and equips everyone.** Empty workplaces are filled from the jobless, tavern visitors are recruited while beds are free, and workers get tools and armour matched to the colony's stage. Every ten seconds the tools of a citizen's own kit, and a guard's armour, are swapped for better ones as soon as the village can make them and his workplace allows them; the old piece is thrown away, enchanted or not, and an empty armour slot is not filled. A tool he only picked up off the ground is left alone. Couriers, the blacksmith and the undertaker keep what they carry, and a builder keeps the tools a blueprint needs for its item frames and armour stands.
- **Feeds and heals.** The hungry are fed and the sick are cured from the colony's stores, or from the treasury when the stores run short.
- **Keeps the warehouse stocked** with staples, destroys the junk that piles up, and teaches the crafters their recipes.
- **Outfits each workplace:** fields for the farm and the plantation, animals for the ranchers, trained mounts for the Stable, a bee nest, a pond for the fisher, a grove for the forester.
- **Researches.** The university works through MineColonies' research tree on its own, unlocking the buildings the plan needs next, then the guards' research. Mounted Combat, which unlocks the Stable, is researched in every colony, so in a style without a Stable you can place one from another style yourself; the mount researches are taken up only where the colony's style pack ships a Stable or one already stands, and each waits for its buildings: Barding, Improved Barding and Advanced Barding (mount armour) need the Stable and the Blacksmith at 3, 4 and 5, and the mounts' health researches also need a Hospital of level 2: a standing Hospital and a standing Blacksmith are upgraded early, ahead of the guard towers, until each has the level the colony's research needs (the Hospital 2 for the mounts' health and 3 for Vaccines, the Blacksmith up to 5 for the mount armour and some technology researches) or reaches the Town Hall's level, so the Blacksmith is usually raised with the Town Hall, one upgrade a level, and the mount armour researches can begin as the Stable rises; the two top ones, Bucephalus and Master Barding, are researched only if an operator lists one in `research.capstones`. The Barracks and the Blacksmith's Hut come unlocked from the start, so the guards get iron gear early.
- **Repairs.** A building that lost eight or more blocks to a raid or an explosion is restored from its blueprint. No builder needed. A building that stands mostly in other blocks than its blueprint's is left as it stands until a builder builds or repairs it: an abandoned colony's Town Hall of another design, or a building you stopped half-way through an upgrade by cancelling its order.
- **Keeps the grounds.** Doors dug free, stray trees felled, deadly drops and lava made safe, berry bushes cleared, and a small hole in the village that citizens keep getting stuck in filled (see *When something looks wrong*).
- **Defends.** Guard towers go up where buildings stand unguarded, up to twenty. The guards are kept at three fighters to one archer with a druid for every four, and more barracks are built as they need room. Where the colony's style pack ships a Stable, one is built for **cavalry** when its turn in the plan comes (one of the eight buildings never given up on, above) and upgraded with the Town Hall, as the guard towers are, with one rider seat and two trained mounts for every level of the Stable: its Stablemaster is handed leather horse armour, which readies a mount at once; each rider gets a spear and a mount; and once a day a mount left standing outside is brought back to its stall and one that died is replaced. The mounts wear the armour of the colony's barding research, leather, then iron, then diamond, bought by the treasury in Progression Mode (the colony's **Barding armour** switch takes it off). The riders patrol the whole town: each ride goes to one of its buildings, they stand there for a minute, ride home, and ride out again two minutes after leaving it (the two minutes are the Stable's own patrol interval, yours to change in its window; the colony's **Cavalry patrols** switch turns the whole patrol off; MineColonies alone would send them only between Stables and Gate Houses, and the growth plan builds no Gate House). Two iron golems watch the Town Hall and one each Blacksmith, re-forged when they fall. An alert horn sounds for everyone online when the village is being killed (unless you muted the colony's chat).
- **Unsticks itself.** A builder whose site stops rising is sent home to find a new way to it. A build that stalls twice is started afresh, by another crew where one is free; one short of the materials for the work in hand, or whose builder cannot be sent home, is started afresh at its first stall. Workers who freeze are reset or rehired.

Every one of these has its own switch. See *Settings and switches* below.

## Money: ColonyBucks

In Progression Mode the colony is not supplied out of thin air. What it cannot make itself is **bought with ColonyBucks** from its treasury: deliveries, building materials, guard gear and gear upgrades, research, and cures when the stores are empty. Recruits, food deliveries and the outfitting of workplaces (fields, ponds, animals, golems) stay free.

**Earning bucks**

- A hostile mob drops a buck one time in thirty when you, a citizen, or something you own (a turret, a tamed wolf) kills it. A citizen's kill goes straight into the treasury.
- Dungeon and other structure chests hold one to four bucks; mineshaft chests none to two; village chests none.
- Every raider killed during a raid pays one buck.
- The **exchange** buys valuables for bucks: two diamonds for one buck, a diamond block for five, sixteen gold ingots for one, one ancient debris for one, a netherite ingot for four. Open it with `/colonyautopilot exchange` or the **Trade** button on the town hall's Exchange tab. It only ever buys. Bucks buy nothing back: only the colony spends them.

**Paying in**

Sneak-right-click the Town Hall block with bucks in your hand. A hopper, a pipe, or the hall's own racks work too.

**When the money runs out**

Nothing happens on credit. Requests, research and cures simply wait, chat says `The colony treasury is empty — waiting for ColonyBucks: 12 requests`, and the moment you pay in, the queue drains. A site waiting for money is never counted as stuck. The colony saves up for research it cannot afford yet and buys it whole.

**Seeing the books**

The town hall's **Exchange** tab (or `/colonyautopilot treasury`) shows the balance, what is waiting, what the colony is saving for, the day's income and spending, the **Trade** button, and a **Re-lay roads** button that redoes every road in the village.

## Keeping your own builds safe

The colony builds around what you make, as long as it knows where that is.

- **Zone Marker.** Right-click two corners with the marker from your kit (or `/colonyautopilot zone wand`) to fence off an area. Inside a zone the colony places nothing, levels nothing, paves nothing, lights nothing, fells nothing and scrubs nothing. Up to 24 zones per colony, each up to 48 blocks on a side. Mark your base before the town reaches it.
- **Left alone on its own:** anything built high over the town, log walls, treehouses, and any block column that holds a chest, a sign or a block nothing can break such as bedrock.
- **Huts you place yourself** are adopted: built, upgraded, staffed and given a road like any other.

## Settings and switches

Open the Town Hall, go to the **Exchange** tab (the coin seal), then **Autopilot settings** (or stand in the colony and run `/colonyautopilot menu`). Every feature of the mod has a row there, and under each name the row says what it changes: **PER-COLONY** rows belong to *this* colony, **GLOBAL** rows to every colony at once. Switches turn on and off, numbers take a value, and **Reset** puts a colony's own value back to the default. Hover a row to read what it does.

Building and upgrading each have their own switch, in the **Construction** section. **Build placed huts** off: the colony still places its huts, and each waits for you to press Build. **Upgrade buildings** off: huts are placed and built to level 1 and every upgrade is yours; the growth plan then waits at each Town Hall step until you raise the hall. Example: you want to decide yourself when the Town Hall goes up a level, so you switch Upgrade buildings off and press its upgrade when you are ready. **Build & upgrade (master)** off stops both, along with the watchdog that restarts stuck builds; in Progression Mode the colony then soon stops placing as well, because every unbuilt hut holds one of its builders' jobs.

The **GLOBAL** rows are the prices, the earning rates, the world tweaks and anything else that affects every colony and every player. On a server only an operator can change them (on the page, with `/colonyautopilot set section.key value`, or in `config/colonyautopilot-server.toml`), so nobody changes the whole server's economy by accident while poking at their own town; everyone else sees them read-only. In single-player you are the server: the host of the world can change them right on the page, cheats on or off, and can use the operator commands too. A single-player world's global values are saved in your game profile's config, so they apply to every world you play in that profile. The master switch is the exception: each world keeps its own, so switching the autopilot off in one world leaves your other worlds as they were.

Commands a survival player actually uses:

| Command | What it does |
|---|---|
| `/colonyautopilot status` | What every colony is doing and waiting for (`status 3` shows colony 3 only; each colony's header shows its number) |
| `/colonyautopilot problems` | The things that need a human |
| `/colonyautopilot treasury` | The balance and the day's ledger |
| `/colonyautopilot exchange` | Sell valuables for bucks |
| `/colonyautopilot chat off` | Silence the colony's chat for yourself |
| `/colonyautopilot expansion off` | No new buildings at all, guard posts included (place your own where buildings stand unguarded); upgrades continue (while Upgrade buildings is on) |
| `/colonyautopilot off` | Hand the colony back to plain MineColonies (trap blocks stay a wall to citizens) |

The full list, including the operator commands, is in [COMMANDS.md](COMMANDS.md).

## Playing with other mods

These are detected automatically. None is required.

- **[Fungal Infection: Spore](https://www.curseforge.com/minecraft/mc-mods/fungal-infection-spore).** The colony gets a grace period of 22 in-game days (about seven hours of play) before Spore creatures spawn by themselves (set it before creating the world). Your own spawn eggs and the `/summon` you type still work during it. Example: you spawn an infected human with an egg to test your wall, and it appears; what it turns into or spawns is held back like the rest, but the creep a creature of yours spreads over the ground is not (a mound you spawn still paints the ground around it). End the grace with `/colonyautopilot set spores.gracePeriodDays 0` (`1` with Spore Inquisition, so its RedNight still comes; in a world younger than one game day that takes effect when the world is a day old). Guards attack the infection on sight. The infection slows citizens instead of killing them, its other effects are cured, the creep is scrubbed off the colony's land and the ground it ate is filled back in, and spore mobs cannot break the colony's blocks.
- **[Spore Inquisition](https://www.curseforge.com/minecraft/mc-mods/spore-inquisition)** (with Spore). The grace counts down in chat, then the RedNight falls at world spawn and the war begins.
- **[TaCZ](https://www.curseforge.com/minecraft/mc-mods/timeless-and-classics-zero)** (the official TaCZ 1.2.0 or newer, or the older unofficial NeoForge port) adds five gun enchantments, and a sixth, Fungicide, when Fungal Infection: Spore is installed too. The colony's guards do not get guns: since 1.4 the gunner guards of the "Compatibility addon for MineColonies" are no longer supported.
- **[K-Turrets](https://www.curseforge.com/minecraft/mc-mods/k-turrets)** (with Spore). Turrets target the whole horde and take no damage.
- **MineTraps.** Its wire, spikes, traps and mines are a wall to your citizens, and to nobody else: citizens walk around them and cannot be pushed or knocked into them, guards still see and shoot through them, and players, raiders, monsters and animals are hurt as before. Leave a gap, a door or a fence gate in a wire line. Example: a ring of barbed wire all the way round the village keeps your own villagers in as well, and one who must cross waits at it until MineColonies moves him. Not covered: a citizen who is already inside wire when it is placed (he is cut until he walks out), one standing in the middle of a patch of wire, and a mounted rider, whose horse walks into wire and carries him in.
- **[Born in Chaos](https://www.curseforge.com/minecraft/mc-mods/born-in-chaos)** and **[Mutationcraft](https://www.curseforge.com/minecraft/mc-mods/mutationcraft)**. Their monsters arrive on a schedule. By default Born in Chaos comes on day 25 and the first mutants on day 30, both counted down in chat, then advanced mutants on day 50, elite mutants on day 70 and the mutant bosses on day 90, which come without a warning; your server's arrivals settings may differ. Until then they do not spawn naturally.
- **[GuideME](https://www.curseforge.com/minecraft/mc-mods/guideme)** gives you the Field Guide, a searchable illustrated book (packs with Applied Energistics 2 have it: AE2 needs it too). **[Patchouli](https://www.curseforge.com/minecraft/mc-mods/patchouli)** or **[Akashic Tome](https://www.curseforge.com/minecraft/mc-mods/akashic-tome)** put the other mods' guides in your kit.

## Style packs

Every style that ships with MineColonies works, and the town is built in the style you pick at founding. Styles designed around fixed building positions, Fortress above all, look best when you place their buildings by hand and let the colony build, staff and upgrade them. Cavalry needs a Stable, and eleven of the 23 styles have one the colony can build on MineColonies 1.1.1420, ten on 1.1.1403 (Minecolonies Original and Fortress among them, Pagoda and Caledonia not): the table in [COMMANDS.md](COMMANDS.md) lists them.

## When something looks wrong

- Start with `/colonyautopilot problems`. Once a day the colony also lists them in chat. Most say exactly what to do.
- A build that is not rising is not necessarily stuck: it may be waiting for money (chat says so) or for a builder to finish another job. Builders who really are stuck are sent home and, if that fails, the job is started afresh, by another builder where one is free (a site short of materials is started afresh at its first stall, without the trip home). You do not need to do anything, except for a decoration or field you placed yourself: if its build stalls twice (once, when short of materials) the order is cancelled, and you place it again.
- A citizen who falls into water whose banks stand a block or more above it cannot climb out by himself. The colony lifts him onto the nearest dry bank: after 30 seconds where the water has no bank low enough, after a minute afloat without getting anywhere (not a guard on Follow within 30 blocks of the player he follows: he swims where that player swims) or two game-minutes afloat at all (not during a raid), at once if his air runs out; a rider goes with his mount, a colony mount left alone is lifted too where a bank beside the water holds it (out of such water, when its air runs out, or after two game-minutes afloat), a mount no bank holds (or out of air in water no bank can be read for) is taken to a dry stall of its built Stable where one has room, its rider still seated, and an iron golem is moved after 30 seconds in any water. Roads now cross open water level with it (only beside a Fisher's Hut's water or the pond dug for it do they still bridge it a block up), and the daily tending of the village ground cuts a step down to the water where a bank has none within a few blocks. Your protected zones and the fisher's pond are left alone. The fisherman is never moved while he fishes at his own spot.
- A citizen stuck on dry land (walled in, in a pit, a ladder gone) is no longer left standing for minutes. MineColonies moves such a citizen to where he was going after two to five minutes; the colony now has it do so about half a minute after his first failed walk (on the test server 29 to 34 seconds, against one to five minutes before). Nobody who works standing in one place is touched: a guard at his post, a worker at his bench, the fisherman, a sleeper. If he is stuck again on the way to the very same place within five game-minutes, that time is left to MineColonies' own pace, so a place he cannot be brought to is not tried every half minute; trapped there again the next morning, he is moved after half a minute again. And a rider thrown off his mount three times within a game-minute at one spot (a gate too low for him) is sent back to his Stable, his mount to its stall. Both follow the colony's **Job watch** switch (on by default), as the citizens' lifts out of the water do; with it off, MineColonies' own slower rescue is all a stuck citizen gets.
- A citizen who keeps getting stuck at one spot is reported with the coordinates, so you can go and fill the hole or bridge the gap. The report says so when there is nothing to fix (a spot inside a building or its building site, a building a builder is still working on, or a walk to a place too far from any player to be awake), and it names a trap block that stands close by: citizens do not walk through traps, so a closed line of them needs a gap, a door or a fence gate.
- A small dry hole in the village where citizens get stuck twice within two game-days (two citizens, or one of them twice) is filled with earth up to the ground round it, so nobody is caught in it again, and is usually not reported (a report that other rescues nearby brought before the fill stays in the list). The village here means beside one of the colony's roads or within 8 blocks of one of its built buildings, and not high over it; the hole is at most 7 blocks across, its top at most 8 blocks over where he stood, walled in on every side, with nothing in it but air or plants. Everyone in it is moved out first: a citizen is sent home, a colony mount to a stall of its Stable (a rider on its back goes with it, still seated), and any other creature, or a citizen whose home cannot be reached, is set on top of the fill. A player in it, on foot, on a mount or in a boat, is never moved: the hole is filled once he steps out. It is left as it is, and reported at the third rescue as above, when it lies in a protected zone, a building's grounds, a building site, a field or another colony's land, lies outside the village, holds water, holds a creature with no room for it on top of the fill, has a door, a gate, a fence or a wall in its side, or opens into a cave, a ravine or other open ground; and nothing is filled while the colony's **Terraform** switch is off. To keep a cellar or a trench you dig in the village, draw a protected zone over it.
- Nobody online: see *An empty server* below. The village stays loaded, but the colony itself rests until its owner or an officer is back.
- Anything else: [open an issue](https://github.com/Back-wards/ColonyAutopilot/issues) with the mod version, the MineColonies version, and the lines from `logs/latest.log` that mention `ColonyAutopilot`.

## For server owners and builders of the mod

- **Server config:** `config/colonyautopilot-server.toml`, every key commented. It is the default for every colony; each colony's own values live in the world save.
- **The growth plan:** `config/colonyautopilot-growthplan.json`. Edit it to change what gets built, in which order, and at how many citizens. It is written once, when it is missing, and never rewritten: after an update of the mod, delete it to get the new version's plan.
- **An empty server.** The village's chunks stay loaded with nobody online (`tweaks.keepVillageLoaded`, on by default), so the village can be attacked while everybody is away. The colony itself rests meanwhile: MineColonies runs a colony only while its owner or one of its officers is online, or a player stands in it, so nothing is built, nobody is hired, the fallen are not replaced and no request is served until then. Whether anything moves there at all is the game's own rule: in a dimension with no player in it, creatures stand still (citizens, guards and monsters alike) unless a chunk in it is force-loaded. With Spore Inquisition installed, as in the modpack, the Overworld always has one: its scripts keep a forced area at world spawn, so an Overworld village keeps moving with you in the Nether or with nobody online. Without it, and in the other dimensions, `/forceload add` on one chunk is enough (in the Overworld Spore Inquisition clears such a chunk every five minutes, which does no harm there). That rule also holds while you are online but in another dimension: without Spore Inquisition, an Overworld village stands still while you are in the Nether.
- **Building from source:** JDK 21, then `./gradlew build` (Windows: `gradlew.bat build`); the jar lands in `build/libs/`. `./gradlew runClient` starts a dev client with MineColonies and its libraries. Optional mods go in `run/mods` yourself.
